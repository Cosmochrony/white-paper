\subsection{Topological Invariants of the Projection Fiber}
  \label{sec:topological-constraints}

  The stability of localized physical descriptions within~$\Pi$ is governed
  by the conservation of topological invariants.
  When an excitation of~$\chi$ admits a closed configuration that is
  non-contractible in the space of allowed configurations, it forms a persistent topological obstruction within the
  admissibility structure.
  Non-contractibility depends on that space: in the fiber $\Pi \cong S^3$ itself every loop is contractible, while
  loops in one circle fiber, or over a trivialising domain of the base, carry an integer winding
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:charge-quantisation}).
  Such configurations cannot be eliminated by smooth deformation without
  violating admissibility constraints.
  These obstructions are described in terms of knot-like topological structures of projected
  configurations (loops non-contractible in the allowed configuration space, and self-linking).
  Once formed, they impose global constraints on the admissibility cascade and
  give rise to long-lived, localized projected configurations.
  Particles correspond to stable topological defects of the projection.

  The homotopy groups of the admissible fiber~$\Pi$ and of its admissible
  base determine which topological invariants are structurally available,
  and hence which physical phenomena can arise.
  Three statements are examined below: the fiber winding, whose reading as electric charge is not derived,
  projective chirality, and a
  computation of~$\pi_2$ on a chosen domain of the base that does not exclude magnetic monopoles;
  their extension to the full effective configuration space~$\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{eff}}$
  remains open (see~\cite{Beau2026k} for the complete treatment).

  \subsubsection*{Fiber winding as a $\pi_1$ invariant, and the missing link to charge}
    \label{sec:charge-quantisation-pi1}

    The Hopf fibration of the projection fiber $\Pi \cong S^3$,
    \begin{equation}
      S^1 \hookrightarrow S^3 \xrightarrow{\pi_H} S^2,
    \end{equation}
    decomposes~$\Pi$ into a circular fiber~$S^1$ and a two-dimensional base~$S^2$.
    The fundamental group of the $U(1)$ fiber is
    \begin{equation}
      \pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}.
    \end{equation}
    Whether a winding number is invariant depends on the configurations and
    deformations allowed.
    For maps of a loop into one circle fiber, the degree $w \in \mathbb{Z}$ is a
    homotopy invariant.
    For maps into the whole fiber $\Pi \cong S^3$ there is no such invariant,
    since $\pi_1(S^3) = 0$: a loop winding around one fiber is contracted
    through loops that leave the fiber, whose base images sweep the whole base,
    and the boundary map $\pi_2(S^2) \to \pi_1(S^1)$ of the fibration is an
    isomorphism.
    Over a domain of the base on which the bundle is trivial, the fiber coordinate
    is global and its winding is invariant under deformations whose base image
    stays in that domain, which is then a choice.

    \begin{proposition}[Integer fiber winding]
      \label{prop:charge-quantisation}
      For configurations with values in a fixed fiber, or in the restriction of
      the Hopf bundle over a trivialising domain of the base, the winding number
      $w \in \pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is invariant under the allowed deformations.
      The admissibility constraint removes configurations exceeding the flux bound
      but does not introduce identifications that would contract the fiber.
      For configurations with values in the whole fiber $\Pi \cong S^3$ there is
      no such invariant.
    \end{proposition}

    The framework reads the winding as electric charge,
    \begin{equation}
      q_{\mathrm{eff}} = w \cdot e, \qquad w \in \mathbb{Z},
    \end{equation}
    with $q_{\mathrm{eff}}$ the circulation of the transverse flux around a loop
    in the emergent description.
    This identification is not derived: $w$ is an invariant of maps into the
    internal fiber, and equating it with a line integral in the emergent
    description requires a specified relation between the fiber and an
    electromagnetic potential there, which is not supplied~\cite{Beau2026k}.
    Charge quantisation in the emergent description is therefore open.

    The winding number~$w$ labels distinct equivalence classes of admissible
    projected configurations in the settings above.
    The organization of the mass spectrum is carried by a distinct abstract
    torsion sector index (Section~\ref{sec:spectral-mass-hierarchy}).
    The energetic cost required to maintain a non-trivial topological
    structure against the global advance of projective ordering is
    perceived, at the effective level, as rest energy~($mc^2$).
    Mass emerges as a spectral and topological consequence of persistent
    structures that are non-contractible in the allowed configuration space,
    not in the fiber~$\Pi$ itself.

    \begin{remark}[Winding conjugation and the spectral pair structure]
      \label{rem:winding-conjugation}
      Charge conjugation corresponds to the reversal $w \mapsto -w$ of the
      winding number.
      In the discrete Weil realisation of $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$,
      the conjugation identity $\rho_{q-c} = \overline{\rho_c}$ shows that
      complex conjugation of the representation is realised by the map
      $c \mapsto q - c$ on the central character.
      In the present setting, the winding number is encoded in the phase of
      the representation, so complex conjugation acts as orientation reversal
      on phase windings, i.e.\ $w \mapsto -w$.
      The pair $\{c,\, q-c\}$ is therefore precisely the discrete realisation of
      the topological conjugate pair $\{w,\,-w\}$, and identifies the conjugate
      Weil pair as the minimal spectral realisation of a topological charge class.
      This identification is canonical: it follows from the representation
      theory of the Heisenberg group and requires no additional hypothesis.
      Its spectral consequences~--- in particular the doubling
      $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}} = 2\delta_c$~--- are developed in~\cite{Beau2026a21,Beau2026k}.
    \end{remark}

    The standard Dirac argument derives charge quantisation from the existence of
    magnetic monopoles via single-valuedness of the wave function on a surrounding
    sphere~\cite{Dirac1931}.
    The fiber used here is not independent of that structure: the Hopf fibration
    is the principal $U(1)$ bundle over $S^2$ whose first Chern number has
    absolute value one (its sign depends on orientation conventions), and its
    natural connection is the magnetic pole of lowest
    strength~\cite{Trautman1977}, in the global formulation of the monopole by a
    nonintegrable phase factor~\cite{WuYang1975}.
    It is this unit monopole class of the internal base that makes every fiber
    winding contractible in~$\Pi$: a fiber loop of winding~$w$ bounds in $S^3$ a
    disc whose image covers the base $w$ times.
    The monopole in question lives over the internal base~$S^2$, not over a sphere
    in the emergent space.
    Whether charge in the emergent description is quantised, and how that would
    relate to monopoles there, is open.

  \subsubsection*{The open monopole question}
    \label{sec:no-monopoles}

    In the unconstrained Hopf fibration, the base manifold is the full two-sphere~$S^2$,
    with $\pi_2(S^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
    In gauge-theoretic language, Dirac monopoles are classified by $U(1)$ bundles over a sphere
    surrounding the source in the emergent space.
    Whether $\pi_2$ of the internal Hopf base classifies magnetic defects of the projected description
    at all is not derived (see the remark below), so neither the admission nor the exclusion of
    monopoles follows from this base.

    The capacity axiom [A-cap], $|\partial_\tau \chi_v| \le c_{\mathrm{BI}}$, is a hypothesis on the
    discrete per-node relaxation rate~\cite{Beau2026c}.
    It does not bound any continuum field strength, and no bound on the gradient of the fiber phase
    is derived from it; TopologicalInvariants uses such a bound only as a hypothesis~\cite{Beau2026k}.
    Neither selects a region of the base.
    The Hopf fibration is a principal $U(1)$ bundle on which $SU(2)$ acts transitively on the
    base: no point of~$S^2$ is distinguished and no fiber degenerates.
    The bundle is non-trivial and admits no global section; where a local section fails to extend is a
    property of the chosen section, which a gauge transformation can move, not of the bundle.
    The canonical admissible model removes, as a choice, two disjoint open discs around the poles
    of a chosen axis.
    The admissible base is then
    \begin{equation}
      S^2_{\mathrm{adm}} = S^2 \setminus (D^2_N \cup D^2_S) \simeq S^1 \times (0,1),
    \end{equation}
    where $D^2_N$ and $D^2_S$ are open discs around the north and south poles.

    \begin{proposition}[Homotopy of the canonical admissible base]
      \label{prop:pi2-trivial}
      The canonical admissible base $S^2_{\mathrm{adm}}$ is homotopy equivalent
      to $S^1 \times (0,1)$.
      Its homotopy groups are
      \begin{equation}
        \pi_1(S^2_{\mathrm{adm}}) \cong \mathbb{Z}, \qquad \pi_2(S^2_{\mathrm{adm}}) = 0.
      \end{equation}
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{remark}[No derived exclusion]
      \label{cor:no-monopoles}
      Proposition~\ref{prop:pi2-trivial} does not exclude magnetic monopoles.
      An exclusion would require two steps that are not derived: that the admissible base is a
      domain with trivial~$\pi_2$, obtained from the projection structure rather than chosen; and
      that $\pi_2$ of this base, the base of the internal Hopf fibration of the projection fiber and
      not a sphere surrounding a source in the emergent space, classifies the magnetic defects of
      the projected description.
      Assuming both would build the exclusion into the assumptions rather than derive it.
    \end{remark}

    In the standard Dirac~--~'t~Hooft~--~Polyakov framework, monopoles are
    topologically admissible and their absence must be explained dynamically.
    The present framework does not supply a topological alternative: the absence of magnetic
    monopoles is an open question.
    Closed magnetic flux lines (corresponding to $\mathbf{B} = \nabla \times \mathbf{A}_T$)
    carry no magnetic charge and are not affected by this question.
    Deriving the admissible base from the projection structure, and determining whether $\pi_2$ of
    the Hopf base bears on magnetic defects at all, are open problems.

  \subsubsection*{Projective chirality and parity violation}
    \label{sec:projective-chirality}

    The winding number $w \in \mathbb{Z}$ carries a sign determined by the
    orientation of the fiber phase.
    In a parity-symmetric theory, configurations with $w$ and $-w$ would be
    related by a mirror transformation and have equal dynamical weight.
    In Cosmochrony, the projection $\Pi : \chi \to \chi_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is
    non-injective, and this non-injectivity can be chiral.

    \begin{definition}[Projective chirality]
      \label{def:projective-chirality}
      A projection is \emph{projectively chiral} if the effective
      stabilisation rates satisfy
      \begin{equation}
        \Gamma(Q) \neq \Gamma(-Q),
      \end{equation}
      where $\Gamma(Q)$ is the effective stabilisation rate for configurations
      of topological charge~$Q$, and $-Q$ denotes the charge-conjugate
      configuration.
    \end{definition}

    When the projection is chiral, parity is violated: mirror-related
    configurations are not equally admissible.
    Two structural mechanisms within the framework produce chirality selection.

    \paragraph{Spinorial selection.}
      The spectral admissibility programme establishes that the bounded-flux
      constraint selects spinorial ($\mathrm{spin}$-$\tfrac{1}{2}$) representations
      as the most admissible along the chain $Q_8 \subset 2I \subset SU(2)$
      (Section~\ref{sec:spectral-admissibility} and~\cite{Beau2026a1}).
      Spinorial representations carry an intrinsic handedness through the
      double-cover structure $SU(2) \to SO(3)$: the central $\mathbb{Z}_2$
      retained by binary covers is precisely the element that distinguishes
      left- and right-handed spinors.

    \paragraph{Non-injective projection.}
      The non-injectivity of the projection creates a fiber degeneracy:
      multiple $\chi$-configurations map to the same effective state.
      The orientation of the winding within this fiber is not preserved by a
      generic projection, so parity invariance is not automatic.
      A chiral projection selects one orientation over its conjugate, producing
      $\Gamma(Q) \neq \Gamma(-Q)$ without any explicit parity-breaking term at
      the substrate level.

      The maximal parity violation observed in weak interactions (polarised beta
      decay of ${}^{60}\mathrm{Co}$) can be interpreted as the manifestation of a
      maximally chiral projection in the $SU(2)$ sector of~$\Pi$.
      Three levels of status should be distinguished.
      At the \emph{structural} level, the non-injectivity of~$\Pi$ makes
      $\Gamma(Q) \neq \Gamma(-Q)$ possible without explicit parity breaking
      in the underlying admissibility structure: this is established by
      Definition~\ref{def:projective-chirality}.
      At the \emph{mechanistic} level, the spinorial selection of
      Chapter~\ref{sec:spectral-admissibility} provides the concrete selection
      filter: the bounded-flux constraint retains a single chirality sector
      via the double-cover structure $SU(2) \to SO(3)$.
      At the \emph{quantitative} level, $\Gamma(Q)$ is not yet expressed as a
      calculable observable of the spectral programme (e.g.\ via
      $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$ or $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}$ of the O-series);
      the derivation of the full $V{-}A$ structure from the admissibility filter
      remains open.
      The relation between projective chirality and CP asymmetry is further
      developed in Appendix~\ref{subsec:cp-asymmetry-and-chiral-selection}.

  \subsubsection*{Unified topological picture}
    \label{sec:topological-summary}

    The fiber winding and parity violation are read under a single structural principle: the
    effective physics is classified and constrained by the topological invariants
    that the admissibility constraint renders available, and by the orientation
    that the projection selects among their generators.

        $\pi_2(S^2_{\mathrm{adm}}) = 0$ & None derived (monopoles open) & Chosen domain of base \\
        Orientation of $\pi_1$          & Parity violation      & Projective chirality           \\
        \hline

      \caption{Topological invariants of the canonical admissible configuration model

      trivialising domain, and its reading as electric charge is not derived; the second is proved for the chosen
      domain of the base, without a derived physical consequence; their extension
      to~$\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is open.
      The third row is established at the framework level as a structural mechanism.
      See~\cite{Beau2026k} for the complete treatment.}

    \end{table}
