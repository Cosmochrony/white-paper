\subsection{Conditional Admissibility Envelope from a Rate Bound}
  \label{subsec:admissibility-bounded-flux}

  The capacity axiom [A-cap] posits a bound on the discrete per-node relaxation rate,
  $|\partial_\tau\chi_v|\leq c_{\mathrm{BI}}$~\cite{Beau2026c}.
  The ordering parameter $\tau$ is not physical time on the static substrate.
  Applying this bound to an effective oscillatory sector requires an additional dynamical hypothesis
  and a fixed convention relating its evolution parameter to~$\tau$.

  Following~\cite{Beau2026a1}, assume a single linearised mode
  $\chi_v(t)=a_n u_n(v)\cos(\omega_n t)$ of the relational Laplacian, with
  $\lambda_n>0$, and impose the same rate bound in the chosen effective parameter~$t$.
  Define the effective amplitude $A_n=|a_n|\,\|u_n\|_\infty$.
  The per-node bound over a full oscillation then gives
  \begin{equation}
    |\omega_n| A_n \leq c_{\mathrm{BI}}.
  \end{equation}
  If the second-order modal dynamics has linearised dispersion $\omega_n^2\simeq\lambda_n$,
  the leading-order envelope is
  \begin{equation}
    A_n^{\max} = \frac{c_{\mathrm{BI}}}{\sqrt{\lambda_n}}.
    \label{eq:wp-modal-envelope}
  \end{equation}
  This is an envelope for the effective amplitude, not the raw coefficient~$a_n$.
  Neither the oscillatory sector nor its dispersion follows from [A-cap] alone.
  The zero mode is excluded: a rate bound on a constant mode does not limit its amplitude.
  For superpositions the node bound constrains the sum of modal rates; satisfying the separate
  single-mode envelopes does not guarantee joint admissibility.

  Within this specified regime, a smaller positive eigenvalue permits a larger effective-amplitude window.
  A larger window does not determine occupation, saturation, or dynamical dominance of a sector.
  Those conclusions require an independently supplied evolution or selection law.
