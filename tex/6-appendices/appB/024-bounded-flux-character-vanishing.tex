\subsection{Neutrality as a Separate Algebraic Condition}
  \label{subsec:bounded-flux-character-vanishing}

  The conditional effective-amplitude envelope of Section~\ref{subsec:admissibility-bounded-flux}
  does not derive character vanishing.
  For a symmetric generating set $S$ of a finite group, write the Laplacian block as
  \begin{equation}
    L_\rho=|S|I-\sum_{s\in S}\rho(s).
  \end{equation}
  Its average eigenvalue is
  \begin{equation}
    \frac{\operatorname{Tr}L_\rho}{\dim\rho}
    = |S|-\frac{1}{\dim\rho}\sum_{s\in S}\chi_\rho(s).
  \end{equation}
  This is a common eigenvalue only when the block is scalar, for example when $S$ is a union
  of conjugacy classes.
  In such a scalar sector with positive eigenvalue, the conditional envelope is
  \begin{equation}
    A_\rho^{\max}=\frac{c_{\mathrm{BI}}}{\sqrt{\lambda_\rho}}.
    \label{eq:admissibility-envelope-B}
  \end{equation}
  The single-mode and linearised-dispersion hypotheses of Section~\ref{subsec:admissibility-bounded-flux}
  apply to this effective amplitude.
  Vanishing of every $\chi_\rho(s)$ sets this average to $|S|$; it does not minimise it over generating sets.

  Neutrality is imposed as an algebraic condition in the classification of neutral generating sets.
  For the defining two-dimensional $SU(2)$ representation, a neutral element is
  $i\vec u\cdot\vec\sigma$ with $\|\vec u\|=1$.
  For symmetric $S$, its inverse $-i\vec u\cdot\vec\sigma$ is also present, so paired matrices cancel
  and $L_\rho=|S|I$.
  This special identity explains the neutral block; it supplies no general optimisation or physical
  selection theorem from the rate bound.
