\subsection{Spectral Stability and the Emergence of \texorpdfstring{$\hbar_{\mathrm{eff}}$}{ℏeff}}
\label{sec:hbar_eff_derivation}

From the fundamental scales $K_0$ (rate constant, dimension $\mathrm{s}^{-2}$), $\chi_c$ (correlation
length), and $c$ (maximal speed), the natural structural quantum is
$\hbar_\chi \equiv c^3/(K_0\chi_c)$, of dimension $\mathrm{m}^2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$
(Appendix~\ref{subsec:fundamental-quantities}).
Here $K_0$ and $\chi_c$ denote bare substrate parameters.

For a solitonic mode with eigenvalue $\lambda_n$, identifying the rest
energy $E_n = \hbar_{\mathrm{eff}}\,\nu_n$ yields
$\hbar_{\mathrm{eff}}$ as a geometric and spectral quantity.
Its identification with the substrate scale, $\hbar_{\mathrm{eff}} = \hbar = M_\chi\hbar_\chi$, is conditional on a
mass scale $M_\chi$ that this paper does not supply (Eq.~\eqref{eq:hbar-Mchi}).

Measurement fixes the same quantum of action in macroscopic systems.
The flux quantum $h/2e$ is observed in superconducting rings of macroscopic
size~\cite{DeaverFairbank1961,DollNabauer1961}, and the Josephson relation $2eV = h\nu$ holds across macroscopic
junctions~\cite{Josephson1962}.
A scale dependence of the form $\hbar_{\mathrm{eff}} \propto (\chi_c/\ell)^2$, which with
$\chi_c \sim 10^{-18}\,\mathrm{m}$ would reduce the quantum of action by some thirty orders of magnitude at
$\ell \sim 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}$, is therefore excluded, and the suppression of quantum effects at macroscopic scales
is not attributed to a reduction of $\hbar_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
Within a fixed projective epoch $\hbar_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is a single constant
(Appendix~\ref{subsec:renormalization-hbar}).
