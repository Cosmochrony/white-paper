\subsection{Fundamental Quantities}
  \label{subsec:fundamental-quantities}

  \paragraph{\texorpdfstring{$\chi$}{χ} (Chi substrate).}
    The static global representation of the pre-geometric relational structure;
    not an independent primitive of the Cosmochrony framework.
    $\chi$ is a pre-geometric, relational substrate not defined on a pre-existing
    spacetime manifold.
    $\chi$ is atemporally fixed: it does not evolve, relax, or carry a dynamical
    state.
    It supplies the admissibility structure that constrains which sequences of
    effective reconstructions can consistently emerge under the non-injective
    projection~$\Pi$.
    $\chi$ does not encode a prescriptive set of admissible physical states;
    admissibility arises only through projection under finite resolution and
    compatibility constraints.
    Localized, topologically stable configurations of $\chi$ correspond to
    particle-like excitations upon projection.

  \paragraph{\texorpdfstring{$\chi_i$}{χᵢ} (Local configuration).}
    Discrete local degrees of freedom of the $\chi$ substrate, associated with
    vertices of the projective relational network.
    They encode the microscopic relational state prior to any geometric projection.

  \paragraph*{$\chi_c$ (Critical admissibility threshold).}
    A fundamental structural bound limiting local variations of $\chi$.
    It constrains the projection and update process by enforcing causal consistency,
    and underlies all effective speed and action bounds.
    This threshold does not prescribe physical laws, but limits the capacity of
    admissible projected updates.

    \medskip
    \noindent\textit{Dimensional convention.}
    The local bound $|D_{\mathrm{loc}}\chi_{\mathrm{eff}}| \leq c$
    (Eq.~\eqref{eq:relaxation-constraint}) implies that
    $\chi_{\mathrm{eff}}$ carries dimension of length.
    Accordingly, $\chi_c$ carries dimension of length.
    The coupling $K_0$ is the rate constant of the discrete admissibility rule:
    in Eq.~\eqref{eq:D4_Si_Kij} the local slope $S_i = c^{-2}\sum_j K_{ij}(\chi_i-\chi_j)^2$ is dimensionless, so
    $K_0$ has the dimension of an inverse time squared, $\mathrm{s}^{-2}$.
    This is the convention of the whole paper for the substrate constants.
    The continuum equations of Sections~\ref{sec:dynamical-equation-for-the-chi-field},
    \ref{subsec:mass_as_resistance} and~\ref{sec:relax-cascade} and of Appendices~\ref{subsec:stability-analysis}, \ref{subsec:analytical-solutions},
    \ref{subsec:charge-as-torsion} and~\ref{app:hubble_tension} write the gradient bound as $|\nabla\chi| \le c$;
    with $\chi$ a length and $\nabla\chi$ dimensionless, this form assumes the normalization $K_0a^2 = 1$ in units
    with $c = 1$, that is $b = c$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:A12-continuum-bound_v171}.
    The structural quantum of Eq.~\eqref{eq:spectral-invariant},
    \begin{equation}
      \hbar_\chi \;\equiv\; \frac{c^3}{K_0\,\chi_c},
      \label{eq:hbar_chi_def}
    \end{equation}
    then has dimension $\mathrm{m}^2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$: it is an action per unit mass, and
    $\ell_\chi = \hbar_\chi/c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ (Appendix~\ref{subsec:IR}) is a length.

    \medskip
    \noindent\textit{The missing mass scale.}
    Every monomial $c^{a}\chi_c^{b}K_0^{d}$ has dimension $\mathrm{m}^{a+b}\,\mathrm{s}^{-a-2d}$, so no action can be
    formed from $c$, $\chi_c$ and $K_0$ alone.
    The identification of $\hbar_\chi$ with Planck's constant therefore takes the form
    \begin{equation}
      \hbar \;=\; M_\chi\,\hbar_\chi ,
      \label{eq:hbar-Mchi}
    \end{equation}
    where $M_\chi$ is a mass scale that this paper does not supply.
    Equation~\eqref{eq:hbar-Mchi} is a conditional identification: it holds once such a mass scale is constructed
    from the substrate, and choosing $M_\chi$ so as to reproduce the measured value of $\hbar$ would be a fit, not a
    derivation.
    Every statement of this paper that identifies $\hbar_\chi$, or a quantity built from it, with $\hbar$ or with an
    energy is conditional on Eq.~\eqref{eq:hbar-Mchi}.

    \medskip
    \noindent\textit{A dimensionless threshold parameter.}
    A unit-independent characterization of the threshold is provided by the ratio
    \begin{equation}
      \tilde{\chi}_c \;\equiv\; \frac{\chi_c}{\ell_\chi}
      \;=\; \frac{K_0\,\chi_c^2}{c^2},
      \label{eq:chitilde}
    \end{equation}
    with $c_{\mathrm{BI}} = c$ in projectable regimes, which is dimensionless by construction.
    When a numerical value is quoted for $\chi_c$ in heuristic estimates, it refers to
    $\tilde{\chi}_c$ unless it carries a unit of length.

    \medskip
    \noindent\textit{No relation to the gravitational coupling.}
    No relation between $K_0$, $\chi_c$ and $G$ is established in this paper.
    Appendix~\ref{subsec:IR} leaves the Einstein coefficient undetermined, and Appendix~\ref{subsec:stepB-G} states the
    Newton coupling as an open matching problem.
    A constitutive relation of the form $K_0\,\chi_c^2 \sim c^4/(16\pi G)$ is not available as a postulate either: in
    the convention above $K_0\,\chi_c^2$ has dimension $\mathrm{m}^{2}\,\mathrm{s}^{-2}$, whereas $c^4/G$ is a force.
    Gravitational normalization therefore does not fix $M_\chi$, nor the scale at which $\hbar_\chi$ and $\hbar$ can
    be identified.

  \paragraph{\texorpdfstring{$\tau$}{τ} (Operational time).}
    An effective ordering parameter defined from the monotonic succession of
    distinguishable projected configurations, constructed from the effective
    descriptor $\chi_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
    Operational time $\tau$ is not a fundamental temporal coordinate and does
    not exist at the level of the $\chi$ substrate.
    It emerges only in projectable regimes as an operational measure of duration,
    defined through the ordered differentiation of admissible effective states.
    In the spectral admissibility programme, the canonical realization of $\tau$
    is the cumulative projected capacity $I(n)$ (see \S\ref{subsec:key-conceptual-terms}),
    which is strictly non-decreasing along the BFS cascade.
    A perfectly stationary projection would correspond to $\Delta\tau=0$ and
    is therefore excluded by the requirement of projective continuity.

  \paragraph{\texorpdfstring{$c_{\mathrm{BI}}$}{cχ} (Fundamental admissible projective speed).}
    The maximal propagation speed of admissible updates within the effective
    reprojection sequence.
    It represents the fundamental causal bound of the theory, from which the
    effective speed of light emerges.
    It represents the kinematic manifestation of the invariant admissible transport
    bound $b_{\chi}$ in transport-limited regimes.
    The effective speed of light $c$ emerges as its geometric projection.
